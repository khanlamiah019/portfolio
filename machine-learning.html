ESG Risk Assessment Platform for Fintech SMBs

Claude API · MCP Tools · STAC API · Geospatial Data · Neural Networks

Built with a five-person team through the Break Through Tech AI Studio program, in partnership with International Elite Capital. The platform helps small and medium fintech businesses assess environmental, social, and governance risk using geospatial data mapped to SASB standards, work that traditionally takes 10 to 20 hours per manual assessment. drug_sentiment_analysis.exe
NLP · Healthcare

Drug Review Sentiment: From VADER to Fine-Tuned BioBERT

VADER · TF-IDF · XGBoost · Bio_ClinicalBERT (Hugging Face)

Research shows that prescription drug reviews, side effects, and real-world effectiveness are often hard for the general public to judge from free-text reviews alone, and this project is a step toward closing that gap. It's a sentiment pipeline built on the UCI Drug Reviews dataset, roughly 215,000 patient reviews with drug names, conditions, a 1 to 10 rating, and free text. The pipeline moves through several stages: exploratory data analysis, a VADER baseline, TF-IDF features into XGBoost, then a fine-tuned Bio_ClinicalBERT model, followed by phrase-level and polarization analysis. The goal is to compare how positive and negative reviews are phrased, then check how well BioBERT's predictions line up with the star ratings patients actually gave, a gold standard for how well the model interprets real patient language. Everything through the XGBoost stage runs independently of Hugging Face; the BioBERT stage relies on it for the model, tokenizer, training loop, and data handling.

gpt_facial_bias.exe
Computer Vision · AI Ethics

Does GPT-4 Judge Faces Fairly? A CNN + GPT-4 Bias Audit

CNN · Computer Vision · GPT-4 · Bias Auditing

A two-part audit of AI hiring bias. First, a CNN trained to predict facial attributes, age, race, and gender, from a cropped image. Second, those same images are shown to GPT-4, which is asked to judge a person's "eligibility" for various jobs, testing whether its assignments track gender and race rather than qualifications. Across nearly every pairing except female-to-female comparisons, GPT-4 assigned stereotypical jobs to 93 to 100% of the images across different trial types, while consistently describing its own choices as "random" and "fair." It also tended to invent detail that wasn't in the cropped image, like assuming business attire, and it more often described women as having "softer expressions" even when the underlying facial expression was essentially the same across images. As far as I've found, there isn't another project that documents GPT's hiring bias this directly, across both race and gender.
